Lesson 1.4: The Journal and Ledger

[cite: 2]

[cite: 2] A Journal Entry is used in double-entry bookkeeping to officially record a financial transaction. It acts as the business’s diary, recording transactions chronologically as they happen. In every single entry, total debits must equal total credits in at least two accounts.[cite: 2]

[cite: 2]
[cite: 2]
[cite: 2]

[cite: 2] ✏️ Anatomy of a Journal Entry[cite: 2]

[cite: 2]

[cite: 2] Every standard journal entry requires five core components to be legally complete:[cite: 2]

[cite: 2]
[cite: 2]
[cite: 2] 📅 1. Date[cite: 2] The exact day the financial exchange took place.[cite: 2]
[cite: 2]
[cite: 2] 📖 2. Accounts[cite: 2] Debits are written on top. Credits are indented below.[cite: 2]
[cite: 2]
[cite: 2] ➕ 3. Debit Amount[cite: 2] The numeric value recorded in the left-hand column.[cite: 2]
[cite: 2]
[cite: 2] ➖ 4. Credit Amount[cite: 2] The numeric value recorded in the right-hand column.[cite: 2]
[cite: 2]
[cite: 2]
[cite: 2] 📝 5. Description: A brief, simple note added to the bottom explaining the business purpose of the transaction.[cite: 2]
[cite: 2]
